# Portable batch STORM protocol

Use this protocol to turn one topic into a bounded, evidence-backed report
without relying on a particular package or model provider.

## 1. Frame the research brief

Record:

- the exact research question;
- intended audience and output form;
- in-scope and excluded topics;
- source restrictions and recency requirements;
- evidence and citation standard;
- time, source, search, and model-call budgets;
- acceptance and stopping conditions.

Ask for clarification only when an unresolved choice would materially change
the research. Otherwise state reasonable assumptions and proceed.

## 2. Survey seed material

Search broadly enough to learn the vocabulary and likely structure of the
topic. Read a small set of credible seed sources rather than relying only on
search snippets. Extract:

- recurring sections or subtopics;
- named stakeholders and disciplines;
- points of disagreement;
- important time periods, jurisdictions, or systems;
- terminology that should guide later searches.

Store each inspected source with a stable source ID, title, locator, access
time, source type, and available text or evidence spans.

## 3. Discover perspectives

Generate complementary research perspectives from the seed survey. A useful
perspective changes which questions, sources, or tradeoffs are examined; it is
not merely a synonym for another section.

For each perspective, record:

- name and rationale;
- 3-6 specific research questions;
- expected source types;
- known overlap with other perspectives;
- uncertainty or controversy to investigate.

Check the set for coverage and duplication before research begins.

## 4. Conduct perspective-guided research

For each question:

1. Decompose it into targeted searches.
2. Discover candidate sources.
3. Read the most relevant sources within budget.
4. Record atomic claims and supporting evidence spans.
5. Record conflicts, gaps, and confidence.
6. Ask one or more follow-up questions when the evidence changes the
   understanding or exposes an important gap.
7. Stop the thread when additional searches are repetitive or the evidence
   condition is satisfied.

Keep the question ledger and evidence ledger separate. A plausible answer does
not count as researched until its important claims are connected to inspected
sources.

## 5. Reconcile the evidence

Normalize duplicate sources and claims. For competing claims:

- preserve both positions;
- identify differences in definition, population, time, method, or incentives;
- prefer primary or authoritative evidence when appropriate;
- mark the balance of evidence without forcing false agreement.

Reject unknown source references and unsupported quotations.

## 6. Draft and refine the outline

Create a preliminary outline, then revise it against the evidence:

- give important, well-supported findings enough space;
- combine repetitive perspective findings;
- preserve meaningful disagreements;
- remove sections with no evidence unless they are explicitly framed as gaps;
- record which claims or sources each section requires.

The final outline should reflect what was learned, not merely the assumptions
made before research.

## 7. Write from evidence

Write each section using the relevant claims and evidence spans. Preserve
source identifiers until the final citation rendering is complete.

- Distinguish fact, interpretation, and recommendation.
- State dates, units, populations, and denominators when material.
- Attribute disputed findings.
- Avoid citing a source for a broader claim than its evidence supports.
- Include limitations and unresolved questions where they affect conclusions.

## 8. Polish without losing provenance

Improve coherence, transitions, lead material, and duplication only after the
evidence-grounded draft exists. Do not let polishing:

- remove necessary qualifications;
- merge distinct claims under one citation;
- invent a citation for uncited prose;
- change the meaning of a supported claim;
- hide gaps or disagreements.

Validate every citation and required output before declaring success.

## 9. Return durable artifacts

Return or persist:

- research brief;
- perspectives and question ledger;
- source, claim, and evidence-span ledgers;
- conflicts and gaps;
- refined outline;
- draft and final report;
- completion status, budgets consumed, and resumable checkpoint.
